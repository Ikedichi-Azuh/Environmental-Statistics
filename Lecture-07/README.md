# Environmental Statistics
## Lecture 7: Missing Data Imputation
Missing data are a common problem in statistical analysis. Simply removing every observation with a missing value can result in a substantial loss of information and statistical power. This lecture introduces missing-data mechanisms and multiple imputation, with a focus on using chained equations to estimate missing predictor values while retaining as much information as possible.

### Learning Objectives
By the end of this lecture, you should be able to:
- Understand why list-wise deletion can substantially reduce the size and statistical power of a dataset.
- Distinguish between missing data in predictors and missing data in the response.
- Understand the difference between imputation, censoring, and truncation.
- Understand imputation as the estimation of missing values.
- Distinguish between MCAR, MAR, and MNAR missing-data mechanisms.
- Understand the principle of chained equations for estimating missing predictor values.
- Understand why the outcome variable can be included in the imputation models.
- Understand how multiple imputation produces several slightly different completed datasets.
- Understand why the analyses from multiple imputed datasets need to be combined (pooled).
- Understand the distinction between complete() and pool() in a multiple-imputation workflow.
- Understand how imputation contributes additional uncertainty to the final parameter estimates.
- Understand the basic idea of shrinkage as a way of reducing the influence of spurious predictors.
