# Environmental Statistics
## Lecture 10: Recapitulation II
This lecture revisits the main ideas from Lectures 6–9. The emphasis is on connecting simulation-based diagnostics, bootstrap, spatial and temporal autocorrelation, generalized least squares (GLS), and generalized additive models (GAMs). The aim is not to introduce new methods, but to understand when the methods from the previous lectures are useful
and how they relate to one another.

### Learning Objectives
By the end of this lecture, you should be able to:
- Understand when bootstrap methods are useful for estimating uncertainty.
- Explain why the usual predict() function does not necessarily provide the uncertainty associated with all data-dependent modelling decisions.
- Understand the purpose of variograms and correlograms for diagnosing spatial autocorrelation.
- Recognise that spatial autocorrelation is assessed in the residuals of a fitted model.
- Understand how GLS can account for spatial autocorrelation in model residuals.
- Understand how omitted predictors and spatial processes such as dispersal can generate spatial autocorrelation.
- Understand the relationship between temporal autocorrelation and spatial autocorrelation.
- Recognise the ACF as a one-dimensional correlogram for temporal data.
- Understand why GAMs can sometimes reduce residual spatial autocorrelation compared with simpler GLMs.
- Use deviance-based model comparisons and the range of fitted effects as measures of the contribution of a GAM term.
- Distinguish between s(), te(), and ti() when specifying interactions in GAMs.
